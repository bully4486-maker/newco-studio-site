Name the neighbourhood, not just the city

People in Oxford don't search the way a county brochure expects. They search the place they are standing, or the place they are willing to travel to. A page that only says “Oxford” competes with the whole city and still fails the person in Headington who wanted a Headington plumber.

  • Jericho. Shops, food and studios along Walton Street and the streets off it. If that is where you are, say Jericho. “City centre” sends people to a different walk.
  • Cowley and the Cowley Road. These are not the same sentence. A workshop in Cowley and a café on the Cowley Road serve different walks from the centre. Say which.
  • Headington, Marston and Risinghurst. Hospital shifts and Headington's own shops create searches that never include the High Street. List the areas you cover, including Marston if you cross that way.
  • Summertown. Its own high street. A Summertown business buried under “north Oxford” is harder to trust than one that states the parade it sits on.
  • East Oxford and Iffley. Iffley village and the Iffley Road are different again from St Clement's and the Cowley Road. Use the name your regulars use.
  • Rose Hill and Blackbird Leys. If you want customers there, say so. A city-centre tone with no estate named will be read as a centre business.
  • Botley. Most of Botley is over the city boundary, in the Vale of White Horse. Say Botley. Customers there are not looking for a High Street chain.

A tradesperson who works three of these and not the rest should list the three. The £99 page has room for that list, a “from” price, a phone number and a photo of the work. It does not need a map of the whole county.

What belongs on the £99 page

The one-page website is for a small, real offer. The thing you sell. The price, or the price it starts at. The neighbourhood. How to book or phone. A photo of the actual work or the actual stall. Basic title and description, a favicon, a share image, and the files handed over. You pay 50% to start and 50% after you sign it off, by UK bank transfer. You see a preview before the balance. The domain and hosting stay in your name. We don't sell hosting and we don't charge a monthly fee.

The Covered Market is the clearest example. A stall page should name the market, what you sell, which days you are there, and how to find the stall once someone is inside. Hours that change in the university vacations should say so, in your words. We won't guess the term dates for you. A photo of the real stall matters more than a paragraph about Oxford.

Send a larger job through the same quote form and it will be priced on its own, or declined. A checkout, a membership area, several staff logins, or a product site for a funded company is not the £99 page. Oxford has agencies for that work. This studio is the small fixed-price job.

Sites you inherited

A common Oxford brief is a site left by a previous owner, a friend, or a student build that nobody can log into. The symptoms are ordinary: the contact form doesn't send, the menu covers the phone number, last summer's hours are still up, a gallery that used to move now leaves a hole. When the change is small and you can give access, that is a fix from £19. Tell us how the site was built. If we can't repair it properly, the reply says so. You get the price in writing first, and you pay once you have approved the fix.

If the inherited site describes a business you no longer run, a repair is the wrong spend. Replace it with one page that matches what you do now.

FAQ

Oxford questions.

Will you come to the café or the stall to plan the page?

No. The work is remote. There is no Oxford office and no visit. Send the brief on the quote form or by Messenger, and review the page on a link.

I only need the Covered Market hours and a photo of the stall. Is that a £99 site?

Yes, if that is the whole offer: what you sell, which days you are in the Covered Market, how to find the stall, and a real photo. A larger shop or a booking system is a different quote.

Can you rank the page above the university?

No. We don't sell positions, and we have no relationship with the university or the colleges. The page describes your business in its neighbourhood. That is the job.

Someone else built the site and the form doesn't send. Can you take it over?

Often, as a fix from £19, if you can edit the site or give us access and the change is small. Tell us how it was built. If we can't repair it properly, we say so in writing.

We are in Botley. Should the page say Oxford?

Say Botley. Most of Botley sits over the city boundary. Customers searching for a Botley shop are not searching for a High Street chain, and the page should match the search they make.
