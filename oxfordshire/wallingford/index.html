What a Wallingford customer looks up first

People decide before they walk into town. They want the day you are there, a phone number that still rings, and a landmark they can stand next to. A Facebook post in a village group does that for regulars. It does it badly for someone driving in on a Friday who has never been.

Wallingford's Charter Market is in the Market Place every Friday, all day. The Country Market runs on Friday morning in St Mary-le-More, on the same square, and the Local Producers' Market is there on Saturday morning. Those are three different answers to “when are you there?”. A page that says “Wallingford market” and stops is the one that sends a customer on the wrong morning.

  • Name the market and the dayCharter Market, Country Market or Local Producers' Market. Say what you sell, and how to order on a day you are not on the square.
  • Put the hours that match the doorShops on the Market Place, the High Street and St Martin's Street lose the visit when the page and the sign disagree.
  • Give a landmark, not a countyMarket Place, the bridge, or the castle gardens. Say which you are beside. The castle gardens are a place to walk, not a business address we can use.
  • Keep the phone number currentAn old number on a page from years ago is a small fix, not a reason to commission a new site.

Who this is actually for

Market traders

A stall page has one job on a Thursday night: tell someone you are there tomorrow, what you bring, and how to find you in the Market Place. If you also do the Saturday producers' market, or you deliver in the week, the same page can say that. Photos should be of your stall or your produce. A one-page site at £99 is enough when that is the whole offer. Send the days, the products and a couple of photos with the quote form.

Shops that are there all week

Independents in the centre often already have a page someone made once. The useful check is dull: does the phone number work, do the hours match this season, does the menu or the price list stay readable on a phone, does the contact form actually send. When the answer is one broken thing, that is a fix from £19. When the page no longer describes the shop, a new one-page site replaces it.

Trades covering the villages

A lot of the work around here is not in the town centre. Cholsey, Crowmarsh Gifford across the bridge, Brightwell-cum-Sotwell and Benson are different jobs from “Oxford” and from a vague “Oxfordshire”. If you will go to those villages, list them. If you won't, leave them off. Customers hiring an electrician or a gardener are trying to avoid a call-out from the city. The page should settle that in the first screen, with a “from” price if you publish one.

Rooms and day visitors by the river

Guests look the page up before they travel, often on a phone. Two railways get confused. Cholsey is the National Rail station, a short bus or taxi from the Market Place. The Cholsey and Wallingford Railway is the heritage line that runs from that station on its own days. A stay page needs the right one, plus how you would like people to arrive, what the room or the mooring includes, and a photo of the real place. We will not write a review of the town, and we will not invent a rating.

FAQ

Wallingford questions.

Will you come to the Market Place to talk it through?

No. The work is done remotely. Send the brief through the quote form, or on Messenger. There is no Wallingford office and no visit to the stall or the shop.

Can one page cover the Friday market and the Saturday producers' market?

Yes. Say which market, which day, what you sell, and how someone orders between market days. That is a normal one-page job at £99 when it fits on a single page.

I cover Wallingford and the villages. Should the page say Oxford?

Say the places you actually work: Wallingford, and villages such as Cholsey, Crowmarsh Gifford, Brightwell-cum-Sotwell or Benson if those are true. “Oxford” attracts the wrong calls for a village round.

My old site still shows a phone number I no longer use. Is that a new website?

Usually not. A wrong number, a broken link or a page that collapses on a phone is a fix from £19 when the change is small. You get the price in writing first, and you pay once you have approved the fix.

Can you make my stall appear above other Wallingford businesses on Google?

No. We don't sell positions. The page gets a clear title, a description and text that matches the stall or the shop. Where it ranks is not part of the quote.
