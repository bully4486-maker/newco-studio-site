Two Didcot jobs

You have just started, and the only advert is a residents' post

Great Western Park, Ladygrove and Valley Park added streets faster than most new businesses added websites. A dog walker, a nursery, a mobile hairdresser, a cleaner or a takeaway often begins as a message in a local group. That reaches neighbours who already saw it. It does nothing for someone who moved in last month and searches the estate by name.

The £99 one-page website is built for that moment. It needs four things from you: what you do, the prices or a “from” price, where you will actually travel, and a phone number or email you answer. A logo is useful and not required. Photos should be of your work, not a stock picture of a different development. You pay 50% to start and 50% after you sign the page off, by UK bank transfer. You see the page on a preview link before the balance, and you keep the files.

You already trade on the Broadway or in the Orchard Centre

The town centre is a short walk from Didcot Parkway. The Broadway and the Orchard Centre are where a lot of existing sites were built once and then left. The laptop view still looks acceptable. The phone view overlaps the opening hours, the PDF menu cannot be read, or the contact form posts nowhere. Customers coming off a train will not rotate a PDF.

That is often a fix from £19, not a new brand. Send the page address and say what should happen instead. If the site can be repaired properly, the quote names the price and the date. If it can't, we say so in writing rather than patching something that will break again next week. You pay a fix once you have approved it.

Write the page for someone between trains

Didcot Parkway is on the Great Western main line, with local trains between Reading and Oxford as well as longer-distance services. A lot of your customers will open the page on a phone, standing on the platform or walking down Station Road. Put the phone number and the price where that screen can see them. Then say which kind of trip you are.

  • If they come to you, say whether you are on the Broadway, in the Orchard Centre, or somewhere they need a bus for. “Near the station” is only fair if it is a short walk.
  • If you go to them, name the estates and the villages. Great Western Park, Ladygrove, Valley Park, East Hagbourne, West Hagbourne and Blewbury are specific. “South Oxfordshire” is not.
  • If your hours follow the commuter day, say the hours. A page that implies you are open when the shutters are down wastes the visit.

The Didcot Railway Centre is the museum in the engine shed beside the station. It is a useful landmark for directions. It is not a place NewCo Studio works from, and it should only appear on your page if it helps a customer find you.

Employment sites are not the town centre

Milton Park, Harwell Campus and Culham sit close enough that people search “near Didcot” when they leave work. A garage, a lunch place, a barber or a tradesperson who will go there should name the site. A business that only covers the town should not borrow those names to sound larger. The wrong list fills the phone with calls you have to turn down. The missing list hides you from the search those workers actually type.

The same rule applies in reverse for a village business. If you are in Blewbury or the Hagbournes and you also take Didcot work, say both. A page that only says Didcot will be read as a town-centre shop.

FAQ

Didcot questions.

Are you based at Didcot Parkway or in the Orchard Centre?

No. NewCo Studio has no Didcot premises, no unit in the Orchard Centre and no desk at the station. The work is delivered remotely, by the quote form or Messenger.

I have only just started on Great Western Park and I have no logo. Can you still build the page?

Yes, if you can say what you do, where you will travel, and how to contact you. A logo can wait. We won't invent one, and we won't use a photo of a different estate.

Our Broadway site looks fine on a laptop and broken on a phone. Do we need a new website?

Not always. A layout that fails on a phone, a form that doesn't send, or hours that are wrong is a fix from £19 when the change is small. Send the page address and, if you can, a screenshot.

Should the page mention Milton Park and Harwell?

Only if you actually cover them. People leaving a shift search for a service near the place they work. Listing a campus you don't serve creates the wrong calls. Leaving it off, if you do serve it, hides you from the search they make.

Can we meet at Cornerstone to agree the site?

No. We don't hold meetings in Didcot. You get a written quote within 24 hours, you accept it in writing, and you review the page on a link.
